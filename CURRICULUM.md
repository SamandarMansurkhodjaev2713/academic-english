# Academic English — Complete 30-day Curriculum

**Audience:** Academic Support staff at an international university · **Start:** self-reported B1 · **Target:** practical B1+ / foundations toward B2.

Every day includes ten vocabulary items, six professional phrases, a 201–231-word original reading, a dialogue (browser-synthesised for listening), comprehension checks, contextual grammar, interactive exercises, a speaking prompt and a writing challenge.

The estimated 60–75 minutes is a suggested study rhythm, not measured automatically. Learners may repeat any day.

## Module 1 — Communication foundations
*Представление, small talk и ясные вопросы* · Days 1–5

### Day 1 — Introducing Yourself Professionally
**Focus:** Профессиональное знакомство · **Grammar:** Present Simple for roles

**Measurable learning outcomes:**
- Introduce yourself and state your Academic Support role in 45–60 seconds.
- Describe two regular responsibilities using the Present Simple.
- Politely refer an approval decision to the correct department.
- Send a concise follow-up message after meeting a visiting coordinator.

**Core vocabulary:** academic support, coordinate, responsibility, liaise with, assist, colleague, enquiry, department, point of contact, follow up.

**Reading:** A first meeting with a visiting coordinator (201 words).
**Workplace challenge:** Introduce yourself to a representative from a partner university. Explain two tasks you handle and one task that requires another department.

**Speaking task:** You are meeting a new visiting lecturer. Introduce your role, explain your responsibilities, and offer a next step.

**Writing task:** Write a 70–100-word introductory email to a new international colleague. State your role, two responsibilities and how they can reach you.

### Day 2 — Professional Small Talk
**Focus:** Непринуждённый разговор · **Grammar:** Present Perfect for experiences

**Measurable learning outcomes:**
- Open a friendly conversation with an overseas university visitor.
- Ask two culturally appropriate questions about the visitor's experience.
- Use the Present Perfect to ask about visits and experiences.
- Close a brief small-talk exchange naturally before a meeting.

**Core vocabulary:** make conversation, settle in, visit, local area, recommend, travel arrangements, weather forecast, conversation starter, follow someone's lead, wrap up.

**Reading:** Before the international staff workshop (221 words).
**Workplace challenge:** Welcome an overseas colleague before a meeting. Keep a friendly two-minute conversation going and end it politely.

**Speaking task:** You meet a visiting professor outside a workshop. Ask two safe questions and respond thoughtfully to one answer.

**Writing task:** Write a short, warm message to welcome a visiting lecturer who arrives tomorrow. Include useful information without being intrusive.

### Day 3 — Describing Your Responsibilities
**Focus:** Объяснение своих обязанностей · **Grammar:** Present Simple + sequencing

**Measurable learning outcomes:**
- Describe three core tasks handled by Academic Support.
- Explain a request workflow with clear sequencing expressions.
- Distinguish your team's support role from formal decision authority.
- Give a visitor the right contact point and a realistic next step.

**Core vocabulary:** day-to-day, workflow, process a request, refer to, escalate, responsible for, collaborate, verify, turnaround time, within our remit.

**Reading:** Who handles which academic request? (217 words).
**Workplace challenge:** Explain a three-step document request workflow and clarify who has final authority.

**Speaking task:** A new international colleague asks what you handle and what another office handles. Give a clear 45-second explanation.

**Writing task:** Write a short departmental overview for an international staff handbook. Name three duties and one boundary.

### Day 4 — Asking Questions & Clarifying Information
**Focus:** Уточняющие вопросы · **Grammar:** Indirect questions

**Measurable learning outcomes:**
- Ask at least three clear clarifying questions during a call.
- Use indirect questions to sound polite and professional.
- Summarise an unclear request before proposing action.
- Confirm what remains unknown without inventing information.

**Core vocabulary:** clarify, confirm, misunderstanding, rephrase, specific, reference number, spell out, interpret, recap, get back to.

**Reading:** Clarifying a registration question on a call (210 words).
**Workplace challenge:** Handle a fast-paced call about a missing module. Ask three clarifying questions before offering the next step.

**Speaking task:** A partner speaks quickly and uses an unfamiliar abbreviation. Ask for clarification without sounding apologetic or insecure.

**Writing task:** Write a follow-up email that restates a caller’s problem, asks for one missing detail and promises a reasonable next action.

### Day 5 — Everyday University Communication
**Focus:** Повседневное общение в университете · **Grammar:** Modal verbs for polite requests

**Measurable learning outcomes:**
- Handle everyday requests about deadlines and room changes.
- Make and respond to polite requests using modal verbs.
- Prioritise four short workplace enquiries calmly.
- Agree on a specific next step for each request.

**Core vocabulary:** available, arrange, reschedule, notify, urgent, attachment, prioritise, update, check in, pass on.

**Reading:** A normal morning in Academic Support (219 words).
**Workplace challenge:** Handle four short requests in the right order: a deadline, room change, meeting request and partner update.

**Speaking task:** Explain politely how you would prioritise requests from a student, lecturer and partner university.

**Writing task:** Draft a concise message informing students about a changed room and asking them to check the updated timetable.

**Module checkpoint:** independent questions covering the module (available in Practice Hub).

## Module 2 — Academic operations
*Программы, расписания и документы* · Days 6–10

### Day 6 — Academic Programs & Degrees
**Focus:** Академические программы и степени · **Grammar:** Must, need to and can

**Measurable learning outcomes:**
- Explain the difference between programmes, modules and degrees.
- Describe compulsory and optional study components accurately.
- Explain prerequisites to a partner adviser without overstating rules.
- Clarify that institutional programme structures may differ.

**Core vocabulary:** undergraduate, postgraduate, degree programme, module, credit, compulsory, elective, curriculum, prerequisite, academic pathway.

**Reading:** Explaining a study plan to a visiting adviser (214 words).
**Workplace challenge:** Explain a programme with compulsory modules, electives and prerequisites to a partner adviser.

**Speaking task:** A visitor asks whether one of their students can transfer credits. Explain the basics and avoid promising approval.

**Writing task:** Write a short explanation of module prerequisites to a student who wants to enrol in an advanced module.

### Day 7 — Admissions & Enrollment
**Focus:** Поступление и зачисление · **Grammar:** Conditionals for procedures

**Measurable learning outcomes:**
- Distinguish an admission offer from completed registration.
- Explain common application and enrolment steps in sequence.
- Use conditional language to explain what happens when documents are missing.
- Guide a student to an official source for institution-specific requirements.

**Core vocabulary:** admission, application, eligibility, enrollment, supporting document, verify, conditional offer, requirement, missing information, registration status.

**Reading:** An offer is not the same as enrollment (208 words).
**Workplace challenge:** Guide a student with a conditional offer through the difference between acceptance and registration.

**Speaking task:** An international student asks whether they can attend classes immediately after receiving an offer. Explain what must be verified first.

**Writing task:** Write a friendly, accurate reply to a student who uploaded the wrong file in the admissions portal.

### Day 8 — Academic Calendar & Scheduling
**Focus:** Академический календарь и расписание · **Grammar:** Future arrangements: will vs going to

**Measurable learning outcomes:**
- Discuss availability and dates across different time zones.
- Distinguish current arrangements from future intentions.
- Propose two alternatives when an academic timetable changes.
- Confirm a mutually agreed schedule in writing.

**Core vocabulary:** semester, academic calendar, timetable, availability, reschedule, time slot, conflict, postpone, confirm attendance, notify participants.

**Reading:** Finding a workable workshop time (216 words).
**Workplace challenge:** Coordinate a workshop with two lecturers and one participant in another time zone.

**Speaking task:** A professor cannot attend a meeting. Offer two alternatives and explain which details still need confirmation.

**Writing task:** Write an email proposing a new workshop time because of a scheduling conflict. Include full date, time zone and request for confirmation.

### Day 9 — Academic Policies & Procedures
**Focus:** Академические правила и процедуры · **Grammar:** Modals for rules and possibility

**Measurable learning outcomes:**
- Describe academic rules with accurate levels of obligation.
- Explain why a policy exception needs formal review.
- Refer an exception request to the authorised decision-maker.
- Respond to uncertainty without promising approval.

**Core vocabulary:** policy, procedure, academic integrity, attendance requirement, exception, appeal, comply with, discretion, refer to the handbook, formal request.

**Reading:** Explaining an attendance rule without overpromising (218 words).
**Workplace challenge:** Explain a policy and the steps for requesting an exception without making the decision yourself.

**Speaking task:** A worried student asks whether a missed-class exception will definitely be approved. Show empathy and explain the process.

**Writing task:** Draft a clear and empathetic email directing a student to the published attendance appeal procedure.

### Day 10 — Documents & Administrative Requests
**Focus:** Документы и административные запросы · **Grammar:** Passive voice for procedures

**Measurable learning outcomes:**
- Differentiate transcripts, certificates and verification letters.
- Describe a secure document request process using passive structures.
- Check consent and responsible offices before sharing records.
- Explain processing stages and realistic next actions.

**Core vocabulary:** transcript, certificate, academic record, authenticate, certified copy, processing time, request form, authorised recipient, issue a document, track a request.

**Reading:** A partner requests a student's transcript (212 words).
**Workplace challenge:** Explain a secure transcript request process to an international partner and distinguish three document types.

**Speaking task:** A partner asks you to send a student's academic record to an unverified address. Explain the approved process politely.

**Writing task:** Write a clear email explaining how a student can request a certificate of enrollment.

**Module checkpoint:** independent questions covering the module (available in Practice Hub).

## Module 3 — Student academic support
*Поддержка, оценки и сложные ситуации* · Days 11–15

### Day 11 — Student Questions & Requests
**Focus:** Запросы студентов · **Grammar:** Open and closed questions

**Measurable learning outcomes:**
- Identify the main purpose of an unclear student enquiry.
- Ask open and closed questions to obtain missing details.
- Direct a student to the appropriate academic team.
- Give two actionable follow-up steps without guessing.

**Core vocabulary:** enquiry desk, request clarification, guidance, relevant, accessible, resolve, refer onward, follow-up message, confidential, next step.

**Reading:** One student, two different questions (211 words).
**Workplace challenge:** Clarify an unclear student enquiry, identify the appropriate team and give two precise next steps.

**Speaking task:** A new student says, “Nothing works on my course page.” Ask useful questions and provide safe guidance.

**Writing task:** Write a concise follow-up with two actions after a student reports a missing online module.

### Day 12 — Grades & Academic Performance
**Focus:** Оценки и успеваемость · **Grammar:** Contrast and caution: however, may, depends on

**Measurable learning outcomes:**
- Explain assessment, grades and academic progress in neutral language.
- Distinguish a query about results from a request to change a grade.
- Use cautious language when systems differ between institutions.
- Direct a concerned student towards authorised feedback channels.

**Core vocabulary:** assessment, coursework, grade, feedback, academic progress, reassessment, grade release, marking, academic standing, result query.

**Reading:** Understanding a result without changing it (209 words).
**Workplace challenge:** Explain a disappointing result, direct a student to feedback and distinguish a result query from a promised grade change.

**Speaking task:** A disappointed student asks you to change a result. Respond professionally, showing empathy and explaining legitimate options.

**Writing task:** Write a respectful message explaining how to find feedback and where to ask about a possible assessment review.

### Day 13 — Deadlines, Extensions & Submissions
**Focus:** Сроки, продления и подача заданий · **Grammar:** First conditional and time clauses

**Measurable learning outcomes:**
- Explain a submission deadline and late-work process clearly.
- Use first-conditionals to describe possible next steps.
- Distinguish requesting an extension from receiving approval.
- Respond professionally to a student's missed deadline.

**Core vocabulary:** deadline, extension, submission, late penalty, supporting evidence, request in advance, mitigating circumstances, eligible, portal timestamp, submit on time.

**Reading:** The deadline is approaching (215 words).
**Workplace challenge:** Explain what a deadline means, how to request an extension and why submission does not guarantee approval.

**Speaking task:** A worried student asks about a deadline tomorrow. Provide calm, clear information and direct them to the correct process.

**Writing task:** Write a 90-word email that explains a deadline and the approved extension procedure without offering an unauthorised promise.

### Day 14 — Handling Difficult Conversations
**Focus:** Сложные разговоры · **Grammar:** Empathetic statements and limits

**Measurable learning outcomes:**
- Acknowledge a frustrated student's feelings without accepting unverified blame.
- Ask questions that establish what can be checked.
- State professional boundaries and escalation options.
- End a difficult conversation with a factual follow-up plan.

**Core vocabulary:** frustrated, acknowledge, concern, remain calm, professional boundary, de-escalate, listen actively, refer to a supervisor, realistic expectation, follow-up plan.

**Reading:** A complaint about a delayed response (216 words).
**Workplace challenge:** Respond to an upset student with empathy, a factual status explanation and a clear follow-up route.

**Speaking task:** A student is angry about a delayed decision and demands certainty. Acknowledge the concern and explain what you can actually do.

**Writing task:** Write a respectful follow-up to a student who complained about slow processing. Avoid defensiveness.

### Day 15 — Academic Problem Solving
**Focus:** Решение академических проблем · **Grammar:** Sequencing and cause

**Measurable learning outcomes:**
- Apply a four-step academic problem-solving sequence.
- Describe causes and consequences without speculation.
- Coordinate missing-record checks across relevant departments.
- Give a student a concise factual progress update.

**Core vocabulary:** identify the issue, root cause, workaround, resolve a case, coordinate a response, priority level, status update, ownership, contingency plan, close the loop.

**Reading:** A missing registration record on orientation day (221 words).
**Workplace challenge:** Use a four-step problem-solving framework for a missing seminar record and communicate a factual update.

**Speaking task:** A student fears losing attendance credit because their name is missing from a list. Explain how you will investigate.

**Writing task:** Write a progress update that separates confirmed facts, actions in progress and the next expected contact.

**Module checkpoint:** independent questions covering the module (available in Practice Hub).

## Module 4 — International communication
*Письма, встречи и университетские партнёры* · Days 16–22

### Day 16 — Professional Emails
**Focus:** Деловая электронная переписка · **Grammar:** Polite email requests

**Measurable learning outcomes:**
- Write a professional email with a meaningful subject line.
- Use polite but direct requests suitable for Academic Support.
- Structure an email around context, action and next step.
- Edit an unclear workplace email into a concise version.

**Core vocabulary:** subject line, recipient, attachment, concise, request, appropriate tone, proofread, closing, thread, action required.

**Reading:** A request that is easy to answer (207 words).
**Workplace challenge:** Edit an unclear email: add a specific subject, one request, relevant context and a realistic deadline.

**Speaking task:** Explain to a new colleague how you would write an email requesting programme information from a partner.

**Writing task:** Write a 90–120-word email asking an international partner to confirm visiting lecturers’ names for a workshop.

### Day 17 — Email Follow-ups & Diplomatic Writing
**Focus:** Напоминания и дипломатичная переписка · **Grammar:** Softening and hedging

**Measurable learning outcomes:**
- Write a diplomatic follow-up to a previous request.
- Use hedging and softening structures appropriately.
- Ask for a status update without suggesting blame.
- Specify a realistic next action and time frame.

**Core vocabulary:** follow-up, pending, gentle reminder, delay, appreciate, status enquiry, diplomatic, acknowledge receipt, inconvenience, revised timeframe.

**Reading:** Following up without sounding demanding (224 words).
**Workplace challenge:** Write a polite reminder that references the original request, gives context and asks for a realistic update.

**Speaking task:** A partner has missed a suggested date. Request a status update without accusing anyone or promising a new deadline.

**Writing task:** Draft a diplomatic follow-up to a partner who has not replied about a document request. Include an optional response date.

### Day 18 — Online Meetings
**Focus:** Онлайн-встречи · **Grammar:** Present Continuous for live situations

**Measurable learning outcomes:**
- Introduce yourself and your role in an online meeting.
- Politely manage microphone and connection difficulties.
- Use present-time language to explain technical issues.
- Distinguish confirmed decisions from unresolved items in a recap.

**Core vocabulary:** join a call, agenda, audio issue, screen sharing, mute, connection, chair the meeting, recap, meeting link, minutes.

**Reading:** A hybrid meeting with an unexpected problem (211 words).
**Workplace challenge:** Join a hybrid meeting, handle a microphone problem and recap confirmed versus pending decisions.

**Speaking task:** You are supporting a virtual academic meeting. Explain a technical problem and guide participants to the correct document.

**Writing task:** Write a five-sentence follow-up after an online meeting: one decision, one pending item, an owner and a next step.

### Day 19 — Participating in Discussions
**Focus:** Участие в профессиональных обсуждениях · **Grammar:** Diplomatic agreement and disagreement

**Measurable learning outcomes:**
- State a relevant opinion in an academic discussion.
- Agree or disagree diplomatically and with a reason.
- Offer one practical alternative during a discussion.

**Reading:** A discussion about orientation support (205 words).
**Workplace challenge:** Contribute to an academic meeting with one opinion, one respectful concern and one workable alternative.

**Speaking task:** A coordinator proposes a very long orientation session. Express a polite concern and propose a practical alternative.

**Writing task:** Write three short discussion contributions: one agreement, one concern, and one alternative proposal.

### Day 20 — Presenting Academic Information
**Focus:** Презентация академической информации · **Grammar:** Signposting a presentation

**Measurable learning outcomes:**
- Present a three-stage academic procedure in two minutes.
- Use signposting language to guide the listener.

- Handle a follow-up question without inventing policy.

**Core vocabulary:** overview, outline, highlight, transition, visual aid, key takeaway, summarise, audience, sequence, question period.

**Reading:** A five-minute briefing about academic requests (211 words).
**Workplace challenge:** Present a three-step academic request workflow in two minutes, including one limitation and an invitation to questions.

**Writing task:** Draft a 100-word script for one slide that explains a three-stage document request process.

### Day 21 — International Partnerships
**Focus:** Международное академическое сотрудничество · **Grammar:** Would, could and require

**Measurable learning outcomes:**

### Day 22 — Cross-cultural Communication

## Module 5 — Advanced workplace communication
*Решения, отчёты и интегрированная практика* · Days 23–30

**Focus:** Объяснение сложных процедур · **Grammar:** Sequencing with once, before and after

**Measurable learning outcomes:**
- Explain a complex academic procedure in numbered stages.
- Use once, before and after to show conditions accurately.
- Identify the documents needed and the authorised decision point.

**Core vocabulary:** step-by-step, prerequisite, exceptional case, verify, submit evidence, conditional, relevant office, processing stage, break down, final approval.

**Speaking task:** Explain what you can now do better, which skill remains difficult, and how you will practise it after this course.

**Writing task:** Write a 120-word professional development plan for the next 30 days with at least three measurable actions.

## Assessment model

- **Placement:** 17 independent selected-response items; optional Day 1 speaking rubric.
- **Daily checks:** reading, listening, grammar and applied exercises.
- **Checkpoints:** Days 5, 10, 15, 22.
- **Final:** 18 independent selected-response items plus separate Day 30 self-reviewed speaking and writing.
- **Scores:** only objectively auto-gradable questions receive numerical correctness scores; open language tasks are not assigned fictitious AI grades.
